# Khan 1 verses: the Lion

Source: Ferdowsi, Shahnameh, Haft Khan-e Rostam, the first labour. Persian text copied from
Ganjoor. Public domain. The English is Amin's own plain translation (draft below, to be
corrected by him); no published translation is used.

Each beyt is one line of Persian in two hemistichs. The `beat` column says when it plays.

## Part 1: the fight (played as the lion dies)

| # | Persian | English (draft) | beat |
|---|---------|-----------------|------|
| 1 | سوی رخش رخشان برآمد دمان / چو آتش بجوشید رخش آن زمان | The lion sprang at radiant Rakhsh, and in that moment Rakhsh boiled up like fire. | fight |
| 2 | دو دست اندر آورد و زد بر سرش / همان تیز دندان به پشت اندرش | He brought both forehooves down on its head, and sank his sharp teeth into its back. | fight |
| 3 | همی زد بران خاک تا پاره کرد / ددی را بران چاره بیچاره کرد | He beat it against the earth until it was torn apart, and by that means left the beast helpless. | fight |

## Part 2: the waking and the scolding (played with Rostam standing before Rakhsh)

| # | Persian | English (draft) | beat |
|---|---------|-----------------|------|
| 4 | چو بیدار شد رستم تیزچنگ / جهان دید بر شیر تاریک و تنگ | When sharp-clawed Rostam awoke, he saw the world had gone dark and narrow for the lion. | wake |
| 5 | چنین گفت با رخش کای هوشیار / که گفتت که با شیر کن کارزار | He said to Rakhsh: you watchful one, who told you to make war on a lion? | scold |
| 6 | اگر تو شدی کشته در چنگ اوی / من این گرز و این مغفر جنگجوی | Had you been killed in its claws, how would I have carried this gorz and this warrior's helm, | scold, pair with 7 |
| 7 | چگونه کشیدی به مازندران / کمند کیانی و گرز گران | the Kayanid lasso and the heavy mace, to Mazandaran? | scold, pair with 6 |
| 8 | چرا نامدی نزد من با خروش / خروش توام چون رسیدی به گوش | Why did you not come to me neighing? Had that neigh reached my ear, I would have risen. | scold |

Notes: beyt 1 names the lion because the preceding lines are not shown, and "sprang" matches
the pounce the game's lion actually does. هوشیار is rendered
"watchful" (awareness of surroundings, standing guard); the bravery is carried by the rest of
the line. Beyts 6 and 7 are one sentence and are always shown as a pair; کشیدی with من as
subject is the classical conditional, first person. In 8, خروش is a loud neigh, not a cry
for help; the closing clause is implied in the Persian and supplied in the English.

## Staging (placeholder art)

1. **Fight verses.** The moment the lion is beaten, the arena holds on the night scene.
   Beyts 1 to 3 appear one at a time on the right third of the screen: Persian first,
   right-aligned, then the English fades in below it smaller. About 3 s per beyt.
2. **Dawn.** The screen warms from the night colour to the dusk colour over 2 s.
3. **Rostam rises.** He stands (placeholder: his body squashes then restores), walks to a
   mark facing Rakhsh, stops. Beyt 4 plays while he walks.
4. **The scolding.** Beyts 5 to 8, each with a small motion so it reads as speech: Rostam's
   body nudges toward Rakhsh on 5 and 8; on 6 and 7 (shown together) the MaceHead visual
   appears in his hand for a beat, since the line is about the gorz; on 8 Rakhsh's head
   (the Mane polygon) dips and lifts.
5. **Title card.** "Khan 1: The Lion" on black with "Press jump to continue". Continue
   restarts to room 1 for now; later it leads to Khan 2.

Jump skips any beat to the next one; holding jump skips to the title card.
